Hospital quality and process optimization

A Donabedian analysis of 100,000 hospital admissions across 10 departments. Use the filters to adjust every chart for patient acuity.

Severity

What the data shows

  • Acuity explains almost all variation. Critical cases stay about 20 days and cost a median of $32.8k, versus 2 days and $551 for Low. A department league table without severity adjustment would mislead.
  • Readmissions occur only in High and Critical cases (about 30%). Low and Medium cases have 0%, so there is no operational readmission signal in those tiers.
  • Critical patients have about 5 times the mortality (10.0% against about 2% in every other tier).
  • Departments perform almost identically after acuity adjustment: LOS 5.3 to 5.5 days, wait 150 to 153 minutes, readmission 7.1% to 8.1%.
  • Wait time shows no association with LOS, cost, readmission or mortality (correlation about 0), and payer mix does not change outcomes. This does not support a "delayed triage worsens outcomes" claim.
  • Recommendation: focus standardization on the High/Critical pathway, where LOS, cost and readmissions concentrate. Do not prioritize by department or payer.
Data limitations. Admission dates increase one day per record (reaching year 2300) and Length_of_Stay_Days disagrees with the date difference in 81% of rows, so LOS is taken as recorded and day-of-week surge analysis is excluded. Cost charts show means, which are right-skewed by Critical cases.
Source: Hospital_Operations_Dataset.csv (100,000 rows). Analysis built with Python and Chart.js.
